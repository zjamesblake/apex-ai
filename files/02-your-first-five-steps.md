# Your First Five Steps

*Do these next week. None of them cost anything.*

---

## Step 1 — Name the person

Think of the one person whose absence next month would hurt the most.

You already know who it is. Everybody lands on their name in about two seconds, which tells you something on its own.

Do not start a programme. Do not start with tools. Start with one name.

---

## Step 2 — Split their week into two piles

Book an hour with them. One hour, not a project. Walk through what they actually did last week, task by task, and sort every item into one of two piles:

**Pile A — genuinely needs what only they know.**
The judgment. The call that requires the years.

**Pile B — everything sitting next to it.**
The admin, the checking, the formatting, the chasing, the re-explaining, the part where they redo something because the input arrived wrong.

### How to run the hour

- **Go by their calendar and their sent items, not their memory.** People describe the job they think they have, not the week they actually had.
- **Ask "why did that come to you?" on every item.** The useful answer is rarely "because it's hard." It is usually "because I'm the only one who can do the next bit, so the whole thing lands on my desk."
- **Do not let them be modest, and do not let them be defensive.** You are not auditing them. You are trying to give them their week back.
- **Write the two piles down where both of you can see them.**

### What you will find

Pile B is always bigger than either of you expected. That is the point.

> The bottleneck is almost never that someone's judgment is rare. It is that their judgment is **bundled** with four hours of work anybody could have done, so the whole bundle can only ever go to one desk.

Once you separate the two, the genuinely scarce part is a much smaller surface than it looked. Small enough to move.

---

## Step 3 — Take the biggest thing in pile B and write it down properly

One thing. Not everything.

"Properly" means: written so that a competent person who is not them can do it and get the same result. Not a summary. Not a Loom. The actual criteria, including the ones that are obvious to your expert and invisible to everyone else.

The fastest way to get it out: **sit with them and ask questions until it is on the page.** They cannot write it themselves, and it is not their fault. When you have done something ten thousand times, most of what you know has gone quiet. You need someone asking "and how did you know to do that?" over and over.

**Definition of done:** somebody else does it, unsupervised, and the output is good. Not "the document exists."

---

## Step 4 — Write your one page

Three parts, one page. Template is in the next file.

- **North star** — the one named, expensive problem you are solving. Not "we're going to use AI."
- **Guard rails** — what you will not do. Rules that make people refuse things.
- **Speed limit** — nobody ships what they can't explain.

---

## Step 5 — Give the team one rule

> **Nobody ships what they can't explain.**

That is your speed limit, and enforcing it is most of the job.

It will feel like you are slowing your best people down. You are not. You are stopping them building things the company cannot own — because the failure that gets you is not someone building the wrong thing, it is someone building past what they understand, and then it breaks and nobody in the building can fix it.

**Verification capacity, not build capacity, sets how fast a team can climb.**

---

## What not to do first

These are the three mistakes, learned the expensive way:

**"Everyone go watch these videos."**
Information without a problem to solve is entertainment. Everyone watches. Nothing changes.

**"Go build leverage in your role."**
No named problem, just go build. And once people can build, they build — because *"what could I build?"* is a far more interesting question than *"what is actually costing us money?"* You end up with a graveyard of half-finished tools and a team that feels extremely productive.

**"We'll reward usage."**
Measure activity and you will get activity. You need people rewarded for **getting rid of work**, not for producing it. Which means you need to be able to see outcomes rather than effort before you ask anyone to automate their own tasks away.

---

## How to run this

**Week one, in order.** None of it needs budget or a tool.

| Day | What | Who | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Name the person | You, alone | 2 minutes |
| Tue | Split their week into two piles | You + them | 1 hour |
| Wed–Thu | Write down the biggest item in pile B | You + them | 2 to 3 hours |
| Fri | Draft the one page | You | 45 minutes |
| Fri | Give the team the one rule | You | 5 minutes |

**Definition of done for the week:** somebody who is not your expert does the pile-B item, unsupervised, and the output is good. Not "the document exists."

**Prompts that do the heavy lifting:** number 2 sorts the two piles from a raw calendar dump. Number 3 runs the extraction interview so you are not trying to interview and take notes at the same time.
